Rule of 55 (Penalty-Free 401k Withdrawals)

If you leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, you can pull funds from that specific employer's 401k without paying the standard 10% early withdrawal penalty. This is a great way to bridge the gap if you retire slightly before 59.5, without locking yourself into a rigid 72(t) schedule.

72(t) SEPP (Substantially Equal Periodic Payments)

IRS Rule 72(t) allows you to avoid the 10% early withdrawal penalty on retirement accounts prior to age 59.5 by taking a series of substantially equal periodic payments (SEPP). Once started, these payments must continue for at least 5 years or until you reach 59.5, whichever is later. Breaking this schedule triggers retroactive penalties!

Sequence of Returns Risk & Cash Buffers

Selling investments during a market downturn early in retirement can permanently damage your portfolio (Sequence of Returns Risk). Building a "Cash Cushion" or CD Ladder (e.g., 3-5 years of expenses) allows you to spend down cash during bear markets and gives your portfolio time to recover.
